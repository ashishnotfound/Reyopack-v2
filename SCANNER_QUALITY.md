# Camera scanning quality

The scanner prefers 1920×1080 at 30 FPS, then 1280×720, then the camera's defaults. These are preferences, not claimed measurements. An explicitly chosen lens is retained throughout fallback. After acquisition, `getSettings()` is checked; an actual stream below 720p gets bounded resolution-upgrade attempts on the same track. Unsupported upgrades leave the working stream usable and show a warning.

Supported continuous autofocus and automatic exposure are requested independently after video playback begins. Neither manual focus distance nor manual exposure is forced. Refocus uses a supported single-shot sweep and restores continuous autofocus after a settling interval when both modes exist; otherwise it requests only a supported automatic mode. Zoom is exposed only if reported by the browser. Control failures do not stop a working decoder. Every control change preserves existing resolution/device constraints: `applyConstraints()` replaces constraints rather than merging them ([API reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaStreamTrack/applyConstraints)). Whether constraint loss caused the physical A55 blur still requires a device measurement.

ZXing and the existing barcode formats are retained. The decoding canvas uses intrinsic `videoWidth`/`videoHeight`, not the CSS preview size, and is resized/redrawn 1:1 when intrinsic dimensions change. The preview uses contain rather than cover, so a label visible to the worker is not cropped just to fill the UI. No thumbnail, lossy recompression, or preview screenshot is used for decoding. Retries remain serial with a 100 ms gap after decoding; valid AWBs are delivered immediately. Rejected non-AWB decodes also wait 100 ms instead of spinning in a zero-delay loop.

## On-device investigation

Open **Scan AWB → Scanner diagnostics** on the affected phone using HTTPS. It reports the camera label/facing, actual track dimensions/FPS, decoder canvas dimensions, supported and reported focus/exposure settings, focus distance/range when available, control failures, attempt counts, decoder outcomes and processing duration. Unsupported settings are shown as unreported, not assumed successful. Counts include only outcomes surfaced by ZXing; some internal format/checksum failures can be surfaced as not-found.

No frames, raw barcode contents, stable device IDs or diagnostics are uploaded/logged. Diagnostics update once per second and at lifecycle transitions. A small original-resolution center-patch sample reports relative edge detail and brightness once per second without changing the full frame used for decoding. Edge detail depends on the scene and is not a calibrated optical focus score.

The dialog scrolls without compressing the video into the controls when diagnostics are expanded. Decoder controls now use ZXing's `scan()` after separately starting the preview: rebuilding the decoder does not dispose the live stream or issue a late torch request. Closing the scanner explicitly releases the track.

## Consecutive-v2 lifecycle

The user's physical A55 observation on the earlier preview was one recognized label, followed by failed recognition while the preview kept moving and looked blurry. Physical diagnostic readings were unavailable. Resolution alone is not treated as proof of sharpness; the optical cause remains unconfirmed.

Enable **Continuous label scanning** before opening the camera. Single-shot mode and the separate **Automatically open camera after packing** option remain available for the original workflow. Continuous mode keeps the dialog, track and decoder alive after a result. A lookup summary provides **View order / Pack** to return to the unchanged full product cards. Scanning never automatically packs or hands over an order.

An in-memory gate allows one lookup at a time and suppresses the same held barcode. Its `finally` releases the lock after success, not-found, rejection or timeout. The next different AWB is eligible immediately after settlement. **Retry this label** permits an explicit repeat. Recovery retains duplicate protection; intentionally closing/reopening permits a deliberate new scan. The selected exposed camera is retained across restarts. Generic rear-camera labels do not justify speculative lens switching; identifiable main/appropriate rear lenses are preferred over known front/auxiliary lenses.

Lookup ownership is separate from camera epochs. A delayed lookup completing after background/resume must update the still-open scanner rather than leave a stale “Looking up” notice. A regression reproduced that stale-state failure before the ownership fix. Intentional new scanner openings still invalidate the old lookup's UI updates.

Diagnostics distinguish track live/muted/ended state, advancing video time, decoder attempt age/running state, lookup/duplicate blocking, accepted and successful-lookup timestamps, and recovery counts. A five-second decoder-attempt stall or decoder exception rebuilds decoding only. A genuinely stalled video clock or ended track can reacquire the selected camera. Recovery is bounded to three attempts per minute before explicit retry is required. Repeated not-found outcomes allow one supported focus retry per camera session, never endless stream restarts/lens hopping. Backgrounding releases the camera; returning resumes the still-open scanner on its selected lens.

For the A55, record the browser/WebView version and photograph the diagnostics (without customer details). Compare actual stream and decoder dimensions before/after Light on and Refocus. Try the main rear lens, diffuse light without glare, and several distances around 15–30 cm; move back if too close to focus. Test repeated scans, portrait/landscape, permission denial, closing/reopening, background/resume, and sustained packing. A sharp preview alone does not prove barcode decode reliability.

## Automated evidence and limits

### Automatic exposed-camera fallback

The scanner first allows a supported focus retry after eight seconds of misses. With **Try another camera automatically if AWB is not detected** enabled, fifteen seconds without a decoded barcode on an actively decoding camera permits another unvisited browser-exposed lens. Identifiable main/rear lenses rank first; known front, macro, ultrawide, depth and telephoto lenses are excluded. Opaque names such as Camera 0 do not identify physical optics; another opaque exposed camera may be tried without claiming it is rear-facing. Browsers exposing one logical camera cannot switch to hidden physical lenses.

At most two alternatives are attempted per dialog opening, with a fresh fifteen-second window on each lens. No switching occurs during an in-flight lookup, stalled decoder or background pause. A visible message reports each attempt. Manual camera selection disables automatic switching until re-enabled; its chosen device remains selected across recovery. The checkbox can disable fallback entirely. Sustained misses do not prove optical blur, and an idle scanner with no label can also reach this timeout. Physical A55 validation remains pending.
